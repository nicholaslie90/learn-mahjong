The 136 tiles

136 tiles that count, plus 8 bonus tiles that don't sit in your hand. Three suits run 1–9 and can form runs. Honours never form runs — only identical groups.

Four copies of each of 34 tiles, and nothing else — no flowers, no seasons. Three suits run 1–9 and can form runs. Honours never form runs — only identical groups.

Three red fives 赤ドラ

One 5 in each suit is printed red — the 5 of characters, bamboo and circles. They play exactly like any other 5, but each one in your hand is worth a bonus han (aka-dora). The dragons are called haku 白, hatsu 發 and chun 中.

組合

What you're building

A winning hand is four sets plus one pair — 14 tiles. Every set is one of three shapes.

Closed kan, open kan

A kan is still one set, with a replacement tile drawn from the dead wall and a new kan-dora flipped. Declare it from four tiles in your own hand and it stays closed — your hand keeps its concealed bonuses. Call it off a discard, or add a fourth tile to a pon, and it is open.

A kong is still one set

Four identical tiles count as a single set, so you draw a replacement tile from the back of the wall to stay at the right hand size. Declaring a kong is optional — it adds a little value and one more tile you can't change.

打牌

How a round plays

Four players, seats named East, South, West and North, turns moving counter-clockwise. East deals and starts with 14 tiles; everyone else holds 13.

Your turn

Draw one tile, then discard one face up in the middle. Your hand is back to 13 and play passes to the right.

Someone else's discard

You may interrupt to claim it — but only to complete a set right now, and you must show the meld face up. Then you discard and play resumes from you.

打牌

How a round plays

Four players, seats named East, South, West and North, turns moving counter-clockwise. East deals and starts with 14 tiles; everyone else holds 13. A game is a hanchan: an East round, then a South round.

Your turn

Draw one tile, then discard one face up. Discards stay in front of you in the order thrown — that river is what everyone reads. Declare a kan or a win instead of discarding when you can.

Someone else's discard

You may interrupt to claim it, show the meld face up, then discard and play resumes from you. Skipped players lose their turn. Any claim except a win opens your hand.

Riichi 立直

With a closed hand that is one tile from winning, you may declare riichi as you discard: put out a 1,000-point stick and turn the discard sideways. You need at least 1,000 points and at least four tiles left in the wall. From then on your hand is locked — draw, and throw back what you drew unless it wins (or is a closed kan that keeps your wait). In return you get a yaku, and the ura-dora under the indicators when you win.

Furiten 振聴

You cannot win on a discard, only by tsumo, in three cases. Permanent: a tile you wait on is in your own river. Temporary: you passed on a winning discard — it lasts until your next discard. Riichi furiten: you passed on a win after declaring riichi, for the rest of the hand.

Dora ドラ

An indicator is flipped on the dead wall; the dora is the next tile in order — 9 wraps to 1, the winds run East, South, West, North, and the dragons run haku, hatsu, chun. Each dora you hold is 1 han. Every kan flips another indicator (kan-dora), and a riichi win also counts the ura-dora beneath. Dora never counts as a yaku — you still need one.

Draws 流局

When the live wall runs out, players who are ready (tenpai) split 3,000 points from those who are not; if everyone or nobody is ready, nothing moves. Some deals are abandoned at once: nine different terminals and honours in your opening hand if you choose to, four of the same wind discarded first time round, four riichi, four kans by different players, or three players calling ron on one tile.

A hanchan 半荘

Everyone starts on 25,000. The dealer seat rotates after each hand — unless the dealer wins or the hand ends in a draw with the dealer ready, in which case the dealer repeats and a honba counter is added. Each honba is worth 300 extra to the winner. Unclaimed riichi sticks wait on the table for the next winner. After the South round, if nobody has reached 30,000, play continues into the West round until someone does.

Who pays

Win on a discard and the discarder pays the whole amount alone. Win on your own draw and all three opponents pay it each — which is why 自摸 hurts.

Payout tables and the exact faan for smaller patterns vary table to table. Agree on house rules before the first deal.

役

Yaku, han and fu

A hand is worth han (doublings) and fu (minipoints). To win at all you need at least one yaku — a scoring pattern. Dora add han but are not a yaku. Closed means you never claimed a discard; a closed kan is still closed.

The one-yaku rule

A complete hand with no yaku cannot win — there is no minimum han, but there must be a yaku. Dora, red fives and ura-dora only add to a hand that already has one. Open tanyao is allowed, and riichi is the easy way to make any closed hand legal.

Counting fu

  • Start at 20.
  • Closed hand won by ron: +10. Tsumo: +2.
  • Pon: 2 open, 4 closed; double for terminals and honours; kan is four times a pon.
  • Pair of dragons, seat wind or round wind: +2.
  • Wait on the middle of a run, an edge, or a single tile: +2.
  • Round up to the next 10.
  • Pinfu: 20 on tsumo, 30 on ron. Seven pairs: always 25.

Base points are fu × 2han+2, capped at 2,000 (mangan). A discarder pays four times base to a non-dealer and six times to a dealer; on tsumo a non-dealer collects base from two players and twice that from the dealer. Everything rounds up to 100. Each cell shows non-dealer on top, dealer below.
