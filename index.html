Four sets, one pair, and a lot of shouting.

That's the whole game. Everything else — the 144 tiles, the claims, the faan — is detail you pick up one round at a time. Start with a finished hand.

牌

The 144 tiles

136 tiles that count, plus 8 bonus tiles that don't sit in your hand. Three suits run 1–9 and can form runs. Honours never form runs — only identical groups.

組合

What you're building

A winning hand is four sets plus one pair — 14 tiles. Every set is one of three shapes.

A kong is still one set

Four identical tiles count as a single set, so you draw a replacement tile from the back of the wall to stay at the right hand size. Declaring a kong is optional — it adds a little value and one more tile you can't change.

打牌

How a round plays

Four players, seats named East, South, West and North, turns moving counter-clockwise. East deals and starts with 14 tiles; everyone else holds 13.

Your turn

Draw one tile, then discard one face up in the middle. Your hand is back to 13 and play passes to the right.

Someone else's discard

You may interrupt to claim it — but only to complete a set right now, and you must show the meld face up. Then you discard and play resumes from you.

Faan, and what it costs

Value is counted in faan. Most tables set a minimum of 3 faan to declare a win, and cap payouts at 10. Faan stack — a concealed self-drawn all-pungs hand collects all three.

Who pays

Win on a discard and the discarder pays the whole amount alone. Win on your own draw and all three opponents pay it each — which is why 自摸 hurts.

Payout tables and the exact faan for smaller patterns vary table to table. Agree on house rules before the first deal.
